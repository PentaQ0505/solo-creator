# Quality control

## Evidence classes

Classify meaningful claims before finalizing:

- **P — Personal fact:** explicitly supplied by the user or visible in provided material.
- **V — Verified public fact:** supported by a current, cited source.
- **I — Inference:** reasonable analysis that must be phrased as a judgment.
- **O — Opinion proposal:** a possible personal position awaiting user confirmation.

Never express V, I, or O as a P claim. Remove unsupported numbers and fabricated quotes.

## Publish-readiness score

Score the draft out of 100:

- Account and audience fit: 15
- Truthfulness and evidence: 20
- Specificity and usefulness: 20
- Distinct angle or personal judgment: 15
- Structure and readability: 15
- Title-content alignment and propagation potential: 15

Decision:

- 80–100: publish-ready after normal user review;
- 65–79: revise once and identify what remains weak;
- below 65: return a stronger outline or request one focused missing input.

Do not display the full scorecard unless requested, but apply it internally.

## Anti-generic review

Revise when the draft contains:

- advice that could fit any creator or industry;
- repeated conclusions with different wording;
- unsupported certainty;
- invented struggle, emotion, income, users, or results;
- a list without a decision, example, or consequence;
- an exaggerated title whose payoff is absent;
- lengthy background before the reader problem appears;
- calls to action unrelated to the content;
- phrases that sound like a guru or advertisement rather than the creator profile.

## Final checklist

- Can the reader identify the problem within the opening section?
- Is there at least one concrete example, artifact, or clearly labeled source?
- Is the creator's judgment visible without pretending certainty?
- Does each section add new value?
- Is the requested platform format complete?
- Are private or unreleased details protected?
- Are current claims cited and dated when necessary?
- Are all placeholders and confirmation notes visible?
